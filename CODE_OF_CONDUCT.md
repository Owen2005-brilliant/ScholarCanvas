# Community Code of Conduct

ScholarCanvas is built for learners, researchers, educators, and open-source contributors with different backgrounds and levels of experience. We want project spaces to be useful, patient, and safe.

## Expected behavior

- Discuss ideas and code with clarity, empathy, and respect.
- Welcome questions from newcomers and explain project-specific assumptions.
- Give actionable feedback about the work rather than personal judgments.
- Respect names, pronouns, identities, privacy, authorship, and cultural differences.
- Credit collaborators and disclose relevant licenses or source inspiration.
- Protect unpublished research, private data, security details, and consent boundaries.
- Accept correction, repair harm where possible, and keep disagreement focused on the project's goals.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, discriminatory language, threats, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Personal attacks, sustained disruption, trolling, or deliberately humiliating feedback.
- Publishing another person's private information or research without permission.
- Misrepresenting authorship, publication status, credentials, or contributions.
- Copying restricted code or assets and presenting them as original work.
- Using project spaces to promote unrelated commercial, political, or abusive campaigns.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, close discussions, reject contributions, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban participants when needed to protect the community. Enforcement decisions should be proportional, documented privately, and centered on safety rather than public punishment.

To report conduct concerns, use the repository's private maintainer contact channel. If the issue involves a maintainer, ask another maintainer to review it where possible. Do not post sensitive evidence in a public issue. Reports will be handled with discretion, though complete confidentiality cannot be guaranteed when action requires limited disclosure.

This policy applies in repository discussions, reviews, community events, and private interactions that directly affect participation in ScholarCanvas.
